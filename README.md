# 🛰️ Outpost Commander

**Team Hope · NASA Space Apps Challenge**

Outpost Commander is a small browser game that teaches how hard it is to keep people alive on the Moon or Mars. You are the commander of a base for **4 astronauts**. You design the base with a limited budget, then try to keep the crew alive for **30 days**.

It is a single HTML file with no dependencies, no install and no internet needed.

## 🎮 How to play

1. **Pick a world:** the Moon 🌙 or Mars 🔴.
2. **Build your base:** you have **100 launch points** to spend on parts. The habitat (20 points) is already included. A rough forecast shows ✅ Safe, ⚠️ Close or ❌ Not enough, so you can fix problems before launch.
3. **Survive 30 days:** press **Next day ▶** (or **Auto-play**) and handle what space throws at you. Keep air, water, food, power and radiation under control and keep the crew healthy.
4. **Read your report:** the end screen shows your score, stars, what went wrong or right, and what you learned.

### Parts you can buy

| Part | Cost | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| ☀️ Solar panel | 8 | Power from sunlight. No sun means no power |
| ⚛️ Nuclear power unit | 18 | Steady power, day and night |
| 🔋 Battery | 6 | Saves power for dark or dusty days |
| 💨 Life support | 10 | Makes air. A second one is a backup |
| 🌱 Greenhouse | 12 | Grows food. Needs power and water |
| 💧 Water extractor | 10 | Makes water from ice. Needs power |
| 🥫 Food crate | 4 | Stored food (about 6 days for the crew) |
| 🛡️ Soil shield layer | 10 | Soil on the roof blocks radiation |

### Surprises during the mission

- 🌑 **Lunar night** (Moon): about 15 days without sunlight.
- 🌫️ **Dust storm** (Mars): the Sun is hidden and solar panels almost stop.
- ☄️ **Solar flares:** a burst of radiation. You choose to **shelter** (safer, but the greenhouse is not tended) or **keep working**.
- ⚙️ **Life support fault:** the main unit breaks. A backup unit saves the day.

## 🎓 What players learn

- ⚡ **Power runs everything.** Air, heat, water and food all need power. If the power stops, the base stops.
- ☢️ **Space has invisible radiation.** Soil on the roof and sheltering during a solar flare keep people safe.
- ⚖️ **You cannot bring everything.** Rockets can only carry so much, so engineers must choose wisely and plan backups.
- 🌙🔴 **Each world is different.** The Moon has a long night. Mars has weak sunlight and dust storms.

The game is built so a young child can follow it. Simple words, traffic-light forecasts, and a "💡 Why it matters" line after each day explain what is happening and why.

## 🌍 Global benefits

Outpost Commander is a free learning tool that anyone in the world can use. Here is the good it can do.

**1. Free and open to everyone**
It is one small file with no sign-up, no install, no cost and no internet needed once downloaded. It runs on an old phone or a basic school computer, so students in places with weak internet or few resources can use it as easily as anyone else.

**2. Makes space science easy to understand**
Ideas like radiation, solar power, life support and mission planning usually feel hard. The game turns them into simple choices with clear results, so children, families and non-experts can understand them by playing.

**3. Inspires the next generation of scientists and engineers**
Playing the commander makes young people curious about space, engineering and problem-solving. A child who enjoys keeping a crew alive today may choose a science or engineering career tomorrow, and the world needs people from every country working on these challenges.

**4. Teaches thinking skills that matter everywhere**
Players learn to:
- Plan with a limited budget
- Weigh trade-offs (you cannot have everything)
- Prepare backups for when things break
- Learn from failure and try again

These skills help in school, work and daily life, not just in space.

**5. Connects space to problems on Earth**
The same ideas apply to life on our own planet:
- **Clean power and storage:** solar panels, batteries and steady power sources, like the energy choices communities face.
- **Water and food:** making the most of limited water and growing food in tough places.
- **Resilience:** planning for storms, power cuts and emergencies.
- **Protecting people:** understanding radiation and how shielding keeps people safe.

**6. Easy to translate and share**
The game uses short, simple sentences and icons, so teachers, students and volunteers can translate it into local languages and use it in classrooms, clubs, science fairs and outreach events.

**7. Supports global space exploration**
Missions to the Moon and Mars are international efforts. A public that understands why they are hard is more likely to support and take part in them, and that helps space exploration benefit all of humanity.

## 📚 Real facts behind the game

- Moon surface radiation is about 1.4 mSv per day (Chang'e-4 lander measurements).
- Mars surface radiation is about 0.7 mSv per day (NASA Curiosity rover RAD instrument).
- A lunar night lasts about 14 Earth days (NASA Moon fact sheet).
- Sunlight on Mars is about 43% as strong as on Earth (NASA Mars fact sheet).
- Dust storms on Mars can block sunlight for weeks. A 2018 global storm ended the Opportunity rover mission.
- NASA's Kilopower project studied small nuclear power units for the Moon and Mars.
- Piling soil (regolith) over a habitat is a studied way to block radiation.

> **Note:** the numbers in the game (the 60 mSv limit, power output, food and water amounts) are simplified for learning. They are **not** real mission values.
